        <div class="list-item">
            <div class="list-text">
                <span class="heading">Jiu-Jitsu might save your life but it will destroy your body</span>
                <p>Jiu-Jitsu is a grappling martial art that uses leverage, joint locks, and chokes to control and submit an opponent. When I showed up to the trial class at the jiu-jitsu (often abbreviated BJJ) club at UCSC, I had no idea just how incredibly addictive this sport would be. I found it to be a perfect mix of problem solving (it's often called "human chess") and athleticism.</p>
                <p>What drew me in even more was the core principle at the heart of the martial art: with knowledge of leverage and technique, the smaller man can defeat the bigger man.</p>
                <h4>The Struggle on the Mats</h4>
                <p>The real reason people say the cliché "Jiu-Jitsu saved my life" isn't (usually) because it literally helped them in a self-defense scenario—it's because of what happens on the mats. In training, you spend a lot of time stuck in terrible positions. You might, for example, find yourself pinned under a much larger opponent. He's putting pressure on your chest to restrict your breathing while actively looking for a submission. You're exhausted, out of air, and just trying to survive.</p>
                <blockquote>The struggle makes the win sweeter, and few things beat the surge you get from finally tapping someone out.</blockquote>
                <p>The real magic happens after practice. Everyday life feels so much easier after doing something so strenuous—both physically and mentally. And the endorphins released after exercise are just the icing on the cake. For a lot of men, Jiu-Jitsu becomes an escape, and it actually works.</p>
                <h4>The Catch</h4>
                <p>The downside is that the sport will eventually take a brutal toll on your body. (Who would've guessed that endless joint locks and being thrown around might not be the best for your health?) It's tragic how something can be so good for your soul yet so bad for your body.</p>
                <p>Really any combat sport is bad for your body. Grappling wrecks your joints and striking arts cause brain damage. Are you willing to pay the price?</p>

                </p>
